# Do and don't examples

## Required image concept

**Do:** start from a `carsxe-feature-artwork` concept — an accepted output for the feature or a newly generated one — before writing SVG or Anime.js code.

**Don't:** start from a screenshot, unrelated SVG, or rough idea. Feed that material into `carsxe-feature-artwork` first.

## Companion handoff

**Do:** carry verified product meaning, brand evidence, semantic composition groups, token roles, exact labels, and avoid rules from the image concept into SVG planning.

**Don't:** sample fixed colors from the bitmap, copy generated text errors, or embed the concept as an SVG `<image>`.

## Product-aware composition

**Do:** identify the feature transformation first, then pick paths and motion that make it visible.

**Don't:** treat “draw-on SVG” as the concept. It is a technique, not a story.

## Developer logging tool

**Do:** draw a server, browser, and framework input into one event route; move a log token through it; reveal the structured trace and connector destinations.

**Don't:** use a glowing AI brain, random terminal gibberish, or unrelated analytics charts.

## Translation tool

**Do:** draw `messages.json`, show strings passing through a translation step, then reveal the repository's real locale codes.

**Don't:** orbit random national flags around a globe or invent supported languages.

## AI feature

**Do:** animate the actual transformation, such as documents entering retrieval and grounded answer fragments becoming available.

**Don't:** use sparkles, humanoid robots, neural-network wallpaper, or pulsing brains unless specifically required by the brand.

## Automotive feature

**Do:** draw a VIN route to real decoded categories or a vehicle timeline to dated history events.

**Don't:** make a car drive in circles when the feature is pricing, identity, or documentation.

## Fintech feature

**Do:** animate a payment or ledger event through real states and expose the final status clearly.

**Don't:** invent dollar amounts, customer names, bank logos, approval rates, or compliance claims.

## Draw-on behavior

**Do:** stagger related strokes so the viewer can understand how the system is assembled.

**Don't:** draw every path at once, animate micro-details before the main structure, or keep erasing and redrawing the asset forever.

## Motion paths

**Do:** use one marker to show a meaningful data route and keep its duration easy to follow.

**Don't:** animate decorative particles with no origin, destination, or role.

## Morphing

**Do:** morph between states that represent a real transformation and share compatible geometry.

**Don't:** morph unrelated icons for visual novelty or accept broken intermediate silhouettes.

## Text

**Do:** keep labels short and based on project terminology, such as `HTTP`, `TRACE`, or `messages.json`.

**Don't:** bake headings, paragraphs, CTAs, fake code, or fabricated metrics into the SVG.

## Page integration

**Do:** match the destination aspect ratio, safe margins, background, responsive behavior, and nearby density.

**Don't:** create a self-contained poster when the page needs a supporting feature illustration.

## Accessibility and lifecycle

**Do:** provide a static completed state, title/description, reduced-motion handling, scoped selectors, and teardown.

**Don't:** leave the image blank without JavaScript, replay on every scroll twitch, or leak observers after navigation.

## Design-system inheritance

**Do:** keep the SVG inline and map fills, strokes, type, radii, and shadows to the project's existing CSS variables or theme object.

**Don't:** introduce a new color, custom font, rounded-card style, or shadow recipe. `@carsxe/design-system` is square (`--radius: 0`) and flat; the only artwork-specific color is the companion's cyan connector.

## Looping

**Do:** default to one play, expose a loop prop or control, add a short hold, and revert the old animation before changing modes.

**Don't:** force endless full-scene movement or ignore reduced-motion preferences.
